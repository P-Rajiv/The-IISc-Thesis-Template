\section{Background}

Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) are deep latent variable generative models that learn a probabilistic mapping between observed data $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ and latent variables $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^d$. Unlike deterministic autoencoders, VAEs impose a structured prior on the latent space, enabling sampling and generation of new images.

\subsection{Latent Variable Model}

We assume the following generative process:

\begin{equation}
p_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{z}) = p_\theta(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{z}) p(\mathbf{z})
\end{equation}

where:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $p(\mathbf{z})$ is a prior over latent variables (typically $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$),
    \item $p_\theta(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{z})$ is the likelihood modeled by a neural network decoder,
    \item $\theta$ denotes decoder parameters.
\end{itemize}

The marginal likelihood of the data is:

\begin{equation}
p_\theta(\mathbf{x}) = \int p_\theta(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{z}) p(\mathbf{z}) d\mathbf{z}
\end{equation}

However, this integral is intractable for deep neural network decoders.

\subsection{Variational Inference}

To approximate the true posterior $p_\theta(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{x})$, we introduce a variational distribution:

\begin{equation}
q_\phi(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{x})
\end{equation}

parameterized by an encoder network with parameters $\phi$.

The objective is to maximize the log-likelihood:

\begin{equation}
\log p_\theta(\mathbf{x})
\end{equation}

Using variational inference, we derive the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO):

\begin{align}
\log p_\theta(\mathbf{x})
&= \mathcal{L}(\theta, \phi; \mathbf{x})
+ D_{KL}\left(q_\phi(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{x}) \| p_\theta(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{x})\right)
\end{align}

Since the KL divergence is non-negative:

\begin{equation}
\log p_\theta(\mathbf{x}) \geq \mathcal{L}(\theta, \phi; \mathbf{x})
\end{equation}

The ELBO is:

\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}(\theta, \phi; \mathbf{x})
&= \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{x})}
    \left[ \log p_\theta(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{z}) \right]
- D_{KL}\left(q_\phi(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{x}) \| p(\mathbf{z})\right)
\end{align}

\subsection{Interpretation of the Objective}

The ELBO consists of two competing terms:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Reconstruction Term}
    \begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{x})}
    \left[ \log p_\theta(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{z}) \right]
    \end{equation}
    Encourages accurate reconstruction of input images.
    
    \item \textbf{KL Regularization Term}
    \begin{equation}
    D_{KL}\left(q_\phi(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{x}) \| p(\mathbf{z})\right)
    \end{equation}
    Forces the approximate posterior to stay close to the prior $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$.
\end{enumerate}

This regularization enforces global smoothness and continuity in latent space but can restrict the representational capacity of the model.

\subsection{Gaussian Posterior Assumption}

Typically, the encoder outputs:

\begin{equation}
q_\phi(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{x}) =
\mathcal{N}\left(\boldsymbol{\mu}_\phi(\mathbf{x}),
\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_\phi^2(\mathbf{x}))\right)
\end{equation}

The KL divergence with standard normal prior has closed-form:

\begin{align}
D_{KL} &= \frac{1}{2}
\sum_{i=1}^{d}
\left(
\mu_i^2 + \sigma_i^2 - \log \sigma_i^2 - 1
\right)
\end{align}

\subsection{Reparameterization Trick}

To allow backpropagation through sampling, we reparameterize:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z} =
\boldsymbol{\mu}_\phi(\mathbf{x}) +
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_\phi(\mathbf{x}) \odot \boldsymbol{\epsilon},
\quad
\boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)
\end{equation}

This enables gradient-based optimization of the ELBO.

\subsection{Limitations of VAEs}

While VAEs enable generative modeling, they exhibit several limitations:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Over-Regularized Latent Space:}  
    The KL term forces latent encodings toward a unimodal Gaussian prior, which may suppress meaningful clustering structure in complex datasets such as chest X-rays.

    \item \textbf{Blurry Reconstructions:}  
    When using Gaussian likelihood assumptions, the decoder optimizes pixel-wise reconstruction loss, often leading to blurry outputs.

    \item \textbf{Posterior Collapse:}  
    The encoder may ignore the latent variable if the decoder is sufficiently powerful.

    \item \textbf{Poor Class Separability:}  
    In medical datasets with subtle pathology variations, enforcing a global Gaussian structure can mix pathological and non-pathological samples in latent space.
\end{enumerate}

Unlike datasets such as MNIST where digit classes form well-separated manifolds, chest X-ray pathologies exhibit subtle intensity variations and overlapping anatomical structures, making it difficult for a standard VAE to produce clearly separated clusters in latent space.
